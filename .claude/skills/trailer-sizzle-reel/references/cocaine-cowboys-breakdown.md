# Case Study: *Cocaine Cowboys* Trailer — Full Breakdown

Source: https://youtu.be/gnO6oDlhfgM (runtime 1:23, ~50 shots).
Breakdown is from an AI scene-by-scene analysis of the video (Higgsfield video analysis), checked against
public coverage of Billy Corben / Rakontur's *Cocaine Cowboys* franchise. Shot timings are ±0.5s. Dialogue is
paraphrased here, apart from a few short fragments; the trailer's actual lines aren't reproduced.

The upload ends on a "GO TO COCAINECOWBOYS.COM" + YouTube SUBSCRIBE end card, so it's a channel/web upload of the
franchise trailer, not a theatrical one. Its content (Griselda Blanco, the Dadeland "Happy Time" party-supplies van,
smuggler pilots, the "it built the city" thesis) matches the original 2006 documentary and its sequels, not the
2021 Netflix series *The Kings of Miami*, which centers on Willy Falcon and Sal Magluta.

## 2. Shot-by-shot map, grouped by beat

### Beat 1: Cold open / atmosphere (0:00–0:06)
- **0:00** Twilight wide shot, a small plane heading for the horizon. Low rumble bed.
- **0:02** Bright, saturated Miami street: a mustached man in a blazer leans on a black Mercedes. A hard mechanical **click** SFX.
- **0:04** Lincoln and a white Econoline van on a tree-lined street (this van comes back at 1:08). Two more clicks.

> **Technique:** The first 6 seconds have no words, just a *world* (plane = smuggling, Miami sun = glamour) and a
> percussive SFX motif (clicks like a gun cocking or a shutter). The van is **planted** here and **paid off** at
> 1:09 when it explodes.

### Beat 2: The invitation story (0:06–0:14)
- **0:06–0:14** First voice: a smuggler tells a first-person story. He's taken to a house, meets a Colombian
  supplier who offers "whatever you want," and says *"show me something."*
- Visuals intercut **1979 Miami skyline archival → extreme close-up of a cigar → close-up of eyes (heartbeat SFX)
  → wide interview frame of the speaker**.

> **Technique:** It opens on a **story in progress** told in plain speech, not on a premise statement. The VO starts
> over B-roll and only later lands on the speaker's face (a J-cut). The ECU → CU → MCU sequence moves the camera
> closer as the story gets more intimate.

### Beat 3: The reveal / payoff of the hook (0:14–0:18)
- **0:14** Hard cut to an older interviewee: he had never seen so much cocaine in his life.
- **0:15** Payoff image: a room stacked floor-to-ceiling with wrapped bricks. Pulsing bed underneath.

> **Technique:** **Setup → punchline in under 15 seconds.** "Show me something" gets answered with an image, not
> words. Ask a question in sound, answer it in picture.

### Beat 4: Scale and stakes (0:18–0:28)
- **0:18** Aerial: a plane landing on a jungle airstrip. Engine flyby SFX.
- **0:21** Young pilot in aviators in a cockpit (archival): the first trip paid **$1.2 million**.
- **0:23–0:28** Two more interviewees deliver the **thesis line**: *"It built the city"*, followed by a list
  (the high-rises and so on).

> **Technique:** **One specific, concrete number** ($1.2M) sells the scale better than an adjective. Then the
> **thesis statement**, the line that turns this from a crime story into a story about a city. It's delivered in
> rhythm by more than one speaker, and every sizzle needs one.

### Beat 5: First music drop — the seduction (0:28–0:34)
- **0:28** A driving electronic beat kicks in **on the cut** to a night skyline.
- **0:30–0:34** Wet neon streets, palm trees, a man driving a convertible at night, a chrome wheel spinning
  (engine-rev SFX). VO: demand was endless.

> **Technique:** The beat drop marks the end of Act 1. Pace jumps to ~1.5s per shot. The palette **flips from warm,
> saturated day to cool blue-green night**: the glamour chapter.

### Beat 6: Turn to danger — the anecdote (0:34–0:42)
- **0:34** Older interviewee by a pool starts a second story: "Flaco" arrives on a motorcycle.
- **0:36–0:41** Dramatized or archival nightclub exterior ("LA JACARANDA" neon): the hit was botched, *"the boss
  wants to meet you."* A face through door slats.
- **0:41** **Black-and-white surveillance/news aerial** of people scattering outside the club. **Gunshots and
  screams.**

> **Technique:** A **second mini-story**, this time violent. Note the **texture shift to B&W surveillance**: a change
> of format tells the viewer "this really happened" and resets the eye.

### Beat 7: The antagonist intro (0:42–0:48)
- **0:42** Investigator interview: names **Griselda Blanco**, "the Godmother."
- **0:44** Car door closes (SFX).
- **0:46** Payoff close-up: a woman in a white hat in the back of a car, **a slight knowing smile**. Musical swell
  and a percussive **hit**.

> **Technique:** **Name first, face second, sting on the face.** The villain reveal gets its own hit point, which is
> the trailer's midpoint.

### Beat 8: Second drop — escalation montage (0:48–1:03)
~15 shots in ~15 seconds, alternating **pleasure ↔ punishment**:
party (headlights on a lawn, laughing in a club) → **door slam** → sweaty driver on a car phone, *"They're
coming!"* → sunset silhouette on a dock → **powerboat at night** → skyline full of cranes → smirking kingpin →
**money blowing through a broken window** → **investigation corkboard + mugshot** → frightened woman in a car →
aerial of sedans at a barn hangar → **gun aimed at the lens + gunshot** → bloodied face → **digging a grave at
night**.

> **Technique:** Each cut lands on a hard SFX (slam, engine, glass, pin, gunshot, shovel). **SFX are the edit
> points.** The two sides of the story are braided together: excess (parties, boats, money) and consequence (cops,
> guns, graves). This is the "everything is happening" stretch, and no shot runs past ~1.2s.

### Beat 9: Title chant (1:03–1:09)
- **1:04** Terrified young face on the grass. VO: *"They called them the Cocaine Cowboys."*
- **1:05** Arrest, pinned to the floor: "Cocaine Cowboys."
- **1:06** Mugshot board with a **camera-shutter click**, which rhymes with the opening clicks.
- **1:08** **The Econoline van from 0:04** (HAPPY TIME COMPLETE PARTY SUPPLIES): "Cocaine Cowboys."

> **Technique:** **The title becomes a rhythmic refrain** said by multiple voices, each over a "consequence" image.
> The shutter click closes the sound loop opened at 0:02.

### Beat 10: The money shot (1:09–1:12)
- The van **explodes** in high-speed slow motion, with glass and a liquor bottle flying. Massive boom.

> **Technique:** The biggest visual spectacle is held back until after the title, and it pays off a prop planted in
> the first 5 seconds.

### Beat 11: The button (1:12–1:17)
- Bearded man in a plaid shirt: someone asked if he was going to kill them. His reply, roughly: not right now, but
  later. Smoking in profile.

> **Technique:** **Release the tension with dark humor.** After the explosion it pulls all the way back to one
> deadpan line of dialogue, which leaves the audience grinning. The button is personality, not plot.

### Beat 12: Final stings + end card (1:16–1:23)
- **1:16** Investigator close-up, heavy bass hit.
- **1:17** Kingpin close-up, last electronic hit.
- **1:19–1:23** Black end card: URL + SUBSCRIBE. Short musical flourish.

## 3. Structural model extracted

```
0:00 ┃ COLD OPEN    atmosphere + SFX motif + plant a prop           (no words)
0:06 ┃ HOOK STORY   first-person anecdote, setup → visual punchline
0:18 ┃ STAKES       one concrete number + THESIS line ("it built the city")
0:28 ┣━ DROP 1 ━━━━ GLAMOUR      music in, palette flips, pace ×1.5
0:34 ┃ TURN         second anecdote, violent; format shift (B&W archival)
0:42 ┃ ANTAGONIST   name → face → sting                                 (midpoint)
0:48 ┣━ DROP 2 ━━━━ ESCALATION   1s cuts, SFX on every cut, pleasure↔punishment
1:03 ┃ TITLE CHANT  title spoken 3–4×, closes the sound motif
1:09 ┃ MONEY SHOT   biggest spectacle, pays off the planted prop
1:12 ┃ BUTTON       deadpan/humorous line, tension release
1:16 ┣━ STINGS ━━━━ 2 close-ups on hits
1:19 ┃ END CARD     title/URL/CTA
```

Proportions: **Setup ~34% · Glamour/Turn ~24% · Escalation + title ~25% · Climax/button/CTA ~17%.**

## 4. Craft principles this trailer demonstrates

1. **Subjects are the narrator.** No announcer VO and no "In a world…". It credits the first-person witnesses,
   which is authentic and cheap, and ideal for docs and reality sizzles.
2. **Anecdotes over exposition.** It's built from 2–3 micro-stories with a setup and a punchline each, not from facts.
3. **Ask in audio, answer in picture.** "Show me something" → room of bricks. "Godmother" → the smile.
4. **One concrete number.** $1.2M on the first trip. Specific numbers beat superlatives.
5. **A thesis line.** "It built the city" lifts it from crime story to a story about a city and an era.
6. **Plant and pay off.** The van at 0:04 explodes at 1:09. The click at 0:02 returns as the mugshot shutter at 1:06.
7. **Music drops = act breaks.** Three distinct musical states. Cut changes land on downbeats.
8. **SFX are punctuation.** Clicks, heartbeat, door slams, gunshots, a pin, a shovel. Nearly every hard cut has a sound.
9. **Accelerating cadence.** Shot length goes 2–3s → 1.5s → ~1s, then stops dead for the button.
10. **Texture contrast.** Warm saturated day / cool neon night / B&W surveillance / clean interview frames.
    Each texture shift wakes the viewer up again.
11. **Braid the opposites.** Glamour and consequence cut against each other, never in separate blocks.
12. **Proximity ladder.** ECU → CU → MCU → wide inside one beat to control intimacy.
13. **J-cuts.** The VO starts before the speaker appears, and the face is the payoff.
14. **Title as chant.** The title is repeated aloud by several voices as a rhythmic device right before the climax.
15. **Hold the biggest spectacle until after the title.**
16. **End on character, not plot.** A darkly funny button makes people want to spend time with these people.

## 5. What it doesn't do (and when you'd want to)
- **No on-screen text** until the end card. For social/muted autoplay you'd add captions and 2–3 word super cards.
- **No release date or platform.** It's a channel upload. A commercial trailer needs a date card.
- **Few faces named.** No lower-thirds. In a pitch sizzle you usually *do* identify talent and credibility.
